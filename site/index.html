01What it does

It isn’t reacting to the music. It’s reading it.

Most music visualisers are a level meter with a shape attached. They bounce when the volume goes up, and that is the whole of the relationship.

ATONAL analyses the whole track before it draws a single frame. It finds the beat grid and where the bars start. It segments the track into intro, breakdown, build, groove, drop. It locates every individual hit and files it by frequency band, so a kick and a hi-hat drive different things. Then it directs the piece from that: the form changes shape at section boundaries and lands on downbeats, the kaleidoscope opens for the loudest passages, the rotation is locked to the tempo rather than to a clock.

The same track always produces the same film. There is no randomness in the direction — a returning chorus comes back as the form it was the first time. You can render it again and get the same result.

02How it works

Three steps, one of which is waiting.

01

Upload a track

MP3, WAV or FLAC. Analysis takes about ten seconds for a four-minute track and reports what it found — tempo, genre, the section map — before you commit to anything.

02

Direct it, or don’t

The default is fully automatic: shape, material, palette, motion and camera are all chosen from the analysis. If you want a say, every one of them is a control — nine forms, nine materials from chrome to glass, six palettes, and a set of film filters. Change nothing and it still works.

03

Pick a format and render

Choose the destination and the length, and it renders offline at full quality — every frame at 60fps with none dropped, however heavy the scene. A fifteen-second clip takes a few minutes. You get an MP4.

05Questions

Before you upload.

Do I own what comes out?
Yes. Exports on a paid credit are yours to use commercially — releases, promos, client work, paid ads. We claim nothing over your music or the visuals rendered from it.
Does the export include my audio?
Not yet. Exports are video only, at exactly the length you chose, so the track lines up when you drop it into an editor or add the original audio in the platform’s own picker. Muxed audio is coming.
How long does a render take?
A few minutes for a fifteen-second clip. It renders offline rather than in real time, which is deliberate: it means every frame is drawn at full quality and none are dropped, regardless of how heavy the scene gets. A real-time capture would judder on exactly the busiest moments.
What kind of music suits it?
Anything with a discernible pulse. It calibrates to each track — the fold opens for whatever counts as loud for that track, not against a fixed threshold — so a quiet ambient piece gets the same range of expression as a hardcore record. Tracks with no rhythmic structure at all get less out of it.
Can I change how it looks?
Every decision the director makes is a control you can override: form, material, finish, lighting, palette, filter, camera, and the rate of each axis of motion. Or leave all of it alone — the automatic result is the point.
