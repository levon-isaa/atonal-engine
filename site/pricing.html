02What a credit buys And what stays free

The reading is the work. The film is free.

Most tools of this kind charge per video, because for them each video is a machine somewhere rendering for minutes. ATONAL renders in your browser, on your own GPU. There is no cost for us to pass on, so we don’t invent one.

What does cost something is the analysis: about ten seconds of real computation to find the tempo, the bar lines, the section map and every individual hit filed by frequency band. That is what a credit pays for, once per track.

03Billing questions
How do I get my credits back on a new machine?
Checkout gives you a render key — one long string, shown on the confirmation page. Copy it: it is saved automatically in the browser you bought from, but nowhere else, and we do not email it. Paste it into the renderer on any other machine and your balance follows you.
There is no account and no password, which is why the key is worth keeping somewhere safe — anyone holding it can spend the credits on it, and for the same reason we stop being able to show it to you a day after the purchase. If you lose it, reply to the receipt Paddle emailed you and we will issue a replacement against that purchase; the balance is on the key, not on you, so it moves with the replacement.
What if an analysis fails?
The credit goes back automatically. A failure on our side is never charged for — the balance is an append-only ledger, so the refund is a visible entry rather than a number being quietly adjusted.
Do credits expire?
No. They sit on your key until you spend them.
Can I use the exports commercially?
Yes, on the 10 and 50 packs — releases, promos, client work, paid ads. We claim nothing over your music or the visuals rendered from it.
Do you keep my music?
The uploaded file is analysed and deleted. What is kept is the analysis itself — tempo, sections, onset times — cached against the file’s fingerprint so that re-uploading the same track costs you nothing.
Refunds?
Unspent credits, within 14 days, no argument. Reply to your receipt. Spent ones are a conversation — tell us what went wrong.
